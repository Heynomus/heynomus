# Research approach

Heynomus follows the public evidence that projects leave behind.

## Begin with identity

Names and tickers can overlap. Project research starts with the exact chain and contract, or a reviewed association with official sources.

A matching name alone is insufficient to establish that two records belong to the same project.

## Read the original source

Public development records, linked websites, and onchain observations provide different kinds of context. Where an observation has a source, readers should be able to inspect it.

## Distinguish claims from observations

A website can describe what a project intends to build. A published release can show that software was released. A code change can show development activity. These observations answer different questions.

A code change does not, by itself, demonstrate that a new product feature is available to users.

## Keep the date

Research should make the observation date visible. A record describes the evidence available at that point in time; it is not a promise that a source is current forever.

## Show the gaps

Unavailable or incomplete measurements remain unknown. They are not silently converted into zero activity, zero usage, or a complete assessment.

## Let readers inspect the evidence

Heynomus organizes public observations to make further research easier. The underlying sources, their dates, and the limits of coverage remain part of the record.
