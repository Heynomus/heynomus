# Research approach

Heynomus follows the public evidence that projects leave behind.

## Begin with identity

Names and tickers can overlap. Research starts with the exact chain and contract, or a reviewed association with official sources. A matching name alone does not establish that two records belong to the same project.

## Read the original source

Public development records, linked websites, and onchain observations provide different kinds of context. Where an observation has a source, readers should be able to inspect it.

## Distinguish claims from observations

A website can describe what a project intends to build. A published release can show that software was released. A code change can show development activity. A code change alone does not demonstrate that a new feature is available to users.

## Keep the date

A record describes the evidence available when it was observed. Source dates remain visible so readers can judge how current each observation is.

## Show the gaps

Unavailable or incomplete measurements remain unknown. They are not silently converted into zero activity, zero usage, or a complete assessment.

## Let readers inspect the evidence

Heynomus organizes observations to make further research easier. Original sources, dates, and coverage limits remain part of the record, including reports requested through X Research.
